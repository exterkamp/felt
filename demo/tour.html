1A board

Everything is drawn on a board: one canvas with one WebGL2 context. Its size, here 480 by 200, is in board units. Positions, card sizes and speeds are all in those units. The canvas is scaled to fit its parent, the way a game canvas is, so the same board is crisp on a phone and on a 4K monitor. The backing store is the board at the screen's pixel ratio.

  • Every card on a board is one instance in one draw call. Each card writes 32 floats (its pose, its art's place in a texture array, its tint) into an instance buffer. The vertex shader bends it, tips it and projects it.
  • A board draws only when something changed. Setting a card's position calls board.touch(). With nothing touched, no frames are drawn and the loop sleeps.
  • Boards don't run their own loop. They are members of the page's one engine(), which owns time, the page lifecycle (hidden, frozen, back from the cache) and the art shared by every board. That is why the nine boards on this page share one loop and one set of card art.
  • Card art is fetched by the cards that need it. A card asks its sheet for its face; the sheet fetches the suit art, the back and the fonts, and renders the court portraits, and the card is not drawn until its sheet is ready, so nothing pops in half-painted. loadCardArt(board) is optional: it only starts that sooner.
Where it lives: src/scene/board.ts (the board), src/gl/renderer.ts and src/gl/shaders.ts (the one draw), src/runtime/engine.ts (the loop), src/art/card-art.ts (the art).


      


      

        
2A card

        

          A card is two things. The data is a plain object,
          { id, rank, suit, faceUp }, which is what a game's rules,
          a save file or a network message deal in; buildDeck()
          makes fifty-two. The sprite,
          new CardSprite(board, card), is that card on a board: a
          node with a pose and a look (width, deck theme, face style), whose
          face is a region of an atlas page painted once and shared by every
          sprite that shows it.
        

        

          Tap a card to turn it over. flipCard rolls it half a
          turn about its own length, lifting it off the felt as it goes. It
          runs on the card's flip channel with a latest
          policy. Tap fast and the taps are not queued up as a string of flips:
          they are debounced into the side you last asked for.
        

        
Where it lives: src/core/cards.ts (the data),
          src/art/card-sprite.ts (the sprite), src/play/flip.ts (the flip),
          src/scene/animator.ts (channels and policies).

3Its pose

        

          A card's place on the board is its pose. Every pose
          field is a property you set directly. Each setter touches the board,
          and the next frame draws the card where it now is. Nothing else is
          needed: no tween, no update call.
        

        
    
          
  • x, y: its centre, in board units. angle:
            its turn in the table's plane, in degrees.
    • 
          
  • z: how far it is lifted towards the camera. A lifted card
            casts a shadow on the felt, further away and softer the higher it is.
    • 
          
  • tilt and turn: rotation out of the plane, in radians,
            about its width and its length. Half a turn shows the other side,
            which is what flipCard animates.
    • 
          
  • bow: a bend down its length, as a fraction of its height,
            used by the riffle shuffle.
    • 
          
  • depth: draw order, not height. Higher draws in front.
    • 
        

        

          The perspective comes from the board's fov (default 45°).
          board.setFov(0) makes the board flat. The same pose then
          draws without depth, and the board's is3d says which it is.
        

        
Where it lives: src/scene/node.ts (the pose),
          src/gl/lens.ts (the projection, which PROJECT in the shader repeats).

        

        

          
          
          
          
          
          
        

        

      


      

        
4A stack

        

          A pile on the table is a Stack. It is data, not a
          node: defineStack({ id, x, y, fan, step, maxSpread, messy, order }).
          It holds no cards. It answers questions about cards: where its
          nth card sits (stackPositions), which one is in
          front (topCardIndex), and where the next would go
          (nextPosition). The game keeps its own array of
          sprites and asks.
        

        
    
          
  • fan runs it 'none', 'down', 'right',
            'left' or 'up', by step units a card.
            With maxSpread set, a pile that would outgrow it squeezes its step.
    • 
          
  • orderStack(sprites, stack) sets each card's depth
            so the right one draws in front: the newest for 'last-on-top',
            the oldest for 'first-on-top'.
    • 
          
  • messy turns each card a little where it lies. The
            amounts come from the card's place in the pile, so a messy pile
            looks the same every time it is laid out.
    • 
        

        

          Because it is pure geometry, the stack code is shared with the
          Phaser engine this one replaced (src/core is that
          engine's copy), and a server can run it without a board.
        

        
Where it lives: src/core/stack.ts (the geometry),
          src/play/order.ts (orderStack).

        

        

          
          
          
          
        

        

      


      

        
5Putting cards on piles

        

          throwCard(board, card, target) flies a card through the
          air to a target. The target can be a point on the felt, or
          { stack, count }, meaning "where this stack's card number
          count goes". It lands exactly there, at that place's angle.
          So when the card joins the array and the pile is laid out again,
          nothing moves: the throw already ended where the layout puts it.
        

        
    
          
  • A throw rises off the felt (arc), can spin
            (spins) and gives a little pop when it lands
            (pop). It resolves when it lands.
    • 
          
  • dealCards is several throws, stagger ms apart,
            each aimed at the next place in the pile.
    • 
          
  • Throws run on the card's move channel and interrupt
            whatever was moving it, so throwing a card that is already in the
            air retargets it rather than queueing.
    • 
        

        
Where it lives: src/play/flight.ts (throwCard,
          dealCards, flightDuration).

        

        

          
          
          
        

        

        

      


      

        
6Dragging

        

          Set card.draggable = true and the board turns pointer
          events on it into dragstart, drag and
          dragend. It picks the node under the pointer, front to
          back, as drawn. The board reports a drag and does nothing to the card:
          the game moves it, so the game decides what can be moved and where
          it goes.
        

        
    
          
  • pointer.refuse() in dragstart turns a drag
            down. Here, only the card in front of a pile comes away.
    • 
          
  • On release, stackUnder(cardRect(at), piles) answers which
            pile the card overlaps most. The card is moved into that pile's
            array, or stays in its own, and both piles are laid out again.
    • 
          
  • On a board with perspective, a dragged card is picked up.
            It rises off the felt and swings with the drag, and its shadow
            falls away. That is an offset on the card, run by springs, and
            the game never sees it. Turn it off with
            pickUp: false.
    • 
        

        
Where it lives: src/scene/board.ts (pointers, picking, drag events),
          src/scene/pick-up.ts (the lift and swing), src/core/stack.ts (stackUnder).

7A hand

        

          A Hand is the other kind of place: cards held in an
          arc. defineHand({ x, y, radius, step, maxSpread, facing })
          is, like a stack, only geometry. handPositions(hand, n)
          places n cards round a grip radius units below,
          step degrees apart, never wider than maxSpread.
          facing turns the whole hand: 180 is a hand across the table.
        

        

          A hand changes shape as it grows, so every card in it moves when one
          joins. layHand tweens each card to its new place. A card
          thrown in aims at the place it will have in the bigger hand
          and is left out of the tween (except) while it flies.
        

        
Where it lives: src/core/hand.ts (the arc),
          src/play/order.ts (layHand).

        

        

          
          
        

        

      


      

        
8Flicking

        

          Let go of a dragged card while it is still moving and it can keep
          going. pointer.velocity is how fast the pointer moved
          over its last tenth of a second, and zero if it had stopped.
          flickCard(board, card, velocity, options) slides it to a
          stop under friction. It hops off the felt if flicked hard, spins if
          held off-centre, and bounces off bounds.
        

        

          magnets are places it may land. Inside a magnet's reach
          the card is pulled in and slowed, and if it stops there it is drawn
          the rest of the way and squared up. onRest(path) says
          which magnet caught it (path.caught). The game decides
          which places are valid; here the one pile takes anything.
        

        

          The whole path is worked out first, in fixed steps of 1/120 s
          (simulateFlick). The same flick always goes the same way,
          and a game can know where a card will stop before it gets there.
        

        
Where it lives: src/play/flick.ts (the physics and the magnets),
          trackVelocity in src/scene/board.ts.

9A table

        

          Sections 4 to 8 did by hand what every card game needs: an array of
          sprites next to each stack, a layout function, depth bands, the
          bookkeeping that lets two throws at one pile land in order, and forty
          lines of drag-and-drop. A Table does all of that
          once. createTable(board) makes one, and
          table.pile() and table.hand() make
          zones: a stack or a hand that holds its own cards,
          lays them out in a depth band of its own, and turns each card that
          lands in it to its side (faceUp).
        

        
    
          
  • table.move(card, zone) is the one way a card changes zone.
            It leaves its old zone at once, so the game can read the new state
            straight away and a second move aims past it. Then it flies to its
            place and turns over as it lands. table.deal(from, zones)
            deals round them like a dealer.
    • 
          
  • Rules live on the zones. accepts(cards, zone, from)
            says what may land there, and drag ('top', 'run',
            'any', false) with canTake says what may be
            picked up. table.draggable() uses both: what you may not pick up
            isn't lifted, the outline only shows over a zone that takes the card, and a drop
            anywhere else goes back. With flick, the zones that accept a card
            are the magnets that draw it in.
    • 
          
  • The rules govern the player. The game's own moves are not
            checked, so a deal can put anything anywhere.
    • 
          
  • table.sync({ zoneId: cardIds }) shows a state that lives
            elsewhere, such as a Simulation, a server or a saved game.
            Whatever isn't where the state says flies there, and nothing else moves.
            The Two seats demo shows each player's view this way.
    • 
          
  • table.on('move' | 'take' | 'drop' | 'tap' | 'refuse') tells the game
            what happened, and table.run(sequence) keeps a deal from being
            started twice.
    • 
        

        

          Below, the reds pile takes only red cards and the blacks only black.
          Drag from the deck, or between the piles. The Solitaire demo is the
          same idea at full size: Klondike as thirteen zones and their rules.
        

        
Where it lives: src/table/table.ts. Under it, everything in
          sections 4 to 8 is still there to use directly.

10The scene around it

        

          This page is one scene:
          defineScene({ name, create(ctx) { … } }). Everything above
          was made through ctx. The nine boards came from
          ctx.board(), the buttons were wired with
          ctx.listen(), and any timers would use
          ctx.delay() or ctx.every(). So when the
          scene ends, for example when you go back to the demos, all of it is
          torn down, newest first, and nothing is left running.
        

        

          A Director moves between scenes. In the demo app it runs
          every demo as a scene on one warm engine, so the next demo starts
          with the card art already painted. For multiplayer, the game itself
          lives outside the boards in a Simulation: plain state,
          changed only by commands at numbered ticks, the same on every copy.
          The boards only animate what a tick changed. See the Two seats demo.
        

        
Where it lives: src/runtime/scene.ts (scenes, the director),
          src/runtime/disposables.ts (teardown), src/runtime/simulation.ts (lockstep games),
          and docs/runtime.md for why.
